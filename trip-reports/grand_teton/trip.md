## The route

The Teton Crest Trail runs the spine of the range on its west side, and the
version we hiked — roughly 31 miles from the Phillips Pass side to Jenny Lake —
packs in most of the trail's greatest hits: Marion Lake, Death Canyon Shelf,
Alaska Basin, Hurricane Pass, and the long descent of Cascade Canyon. The trail
rarely drops below 8,000 feet, so you're living above treeline for most of it,
which is exactly why you come.

## Day 1 — up to Fox Creek Pass

Day one is the honest one. The climb starts almost immediately and doesn't
really let up, and the elevation gain hit harder than the mileage suggested.
The consolation prize is wildflowers — the meadows in the first stretch were
going off, entire hillsides of them with peaks stacked behind.

Water is the thing to plan around here: it was scarce for the first ten miles
until **Marion Lake**, which is also the first real "stop and stare" moment of
the trip. From Marion the trail climbs to **Fox Creek Pass** (~9,600 ft), where
the Death Canyon Shelf spreads out ahead. We made camp near the pass, tired in
the way that only front-loaded elevation makes you.

## Day 2 — Alaska Basin and Hurricane Pass

The shelf is the payoff for day one: a broad limestone bench with a thousand-foot
wall on one side and Death Canyon dropping away on the other. From there the
trail dips through **Alaska Basin** — lakes, granite, and briefly out of the
park — before the climb to **Hurricane Pass** at 10,338 feet, the high point
of the trip. The Grand, Middle, and South Tetons line up right in front of you,
and tiny **Schoolroom Glacier** with its perfect little moraine ring sits just
below the pass.

From there it's all downhill into the South Fork of Cascade Canyon. We camped
next to a creek that ran right through the campsite — falling-asleep-to-water
kind of night.

## Day 3 — out through Cascade Canyon

The last day is a long, gradual walk out beneath the biggest walls of the trip,
with the canyon slowly opening up toward **Jenny Lake**. You can hike around
the lake to the trailhead or catch the shuttle boat across — it runs every
10–15 minutes and after 30 miles it's an easy sell.

## Logistics

Overnights on the Teton Crest require a Grand Teton backcountry permit
(camping zones — we used Death Canyon Shelf and South Fork Cascade Canyon), and
an approved bear canister is required; you can borrow one free at the permit
station. Passes can hold snow well into July, so check conditions before
committing to an early-season date.
