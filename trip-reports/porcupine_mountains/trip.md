## The route

The Porkies are Michigan's largest state park — 60,000 acres, holding the
biggest tract of old-growth hemlock-hardwood forest left in the Midwest — and
this traverse strings together its three best trails. Roughly 22 miles: west
along the **Escarpment Trail** above Lake of the Clouds, down the **Big Carp
River Trail** through the old growth to Lake Superior, then northeast up the
**Lake Superior Trail** along the shore. It never gets above 1,600 feet, but
don't let that fool you — the Porkies make you earn every mile with roots,
rock, and mud.

## Day 1 — the Escarpment in evening light

We started from the Government Peak trailhead on M-107 late in the afternoon,
which turned out to be the trip's best decision. The Escarpment Trail climbs
quickly to a ridge of cliffs and open rock, and by early evening we had the
most famous view in Michigan — **Lake of the Clouds** stretched out below,
ringed by unbroken forest — in soft light with nobody else around. The ridge
is dry the whole way, so we carried the evening's water up with us and camped
at a designated site where the trail begins to bend toward the Big Carp
drainage.

## Day 2 — old growth down to the big lake

Day 2 was the long one. A last morning of cliff-edge views off the escarpment,
then the **Big Carp River Trail** dropped us into the old growth — hemlocks
two people couldn't wrap arms around, with the tannin-stained river winding
brown-gold below the trail. This stretch felt like a different park entirely:
shaded, quiet, green in every direction.

By mid-afternoon the forest opened up and there it was — **Lake Superior**,
flat and enormous, lapping at shelves of dark rock near the Big Carp's mouth.
We turned northeast up the shore and took a designated shoreline site with a
fire ring and log benches facing the water. Falling asleep to Superior's waves
after a day in the hemlocks is the whole reason to route the trip this way.

## Day 3 — thimbleberries and mud

The walk out on the **Lake Superior Trail** was short and comic in equal
measure. August means the thimbleberries are ripe, and for long stretches the
trail is a green tunnel of them — the dog disappeared entirely. It also means
mud: deep, churned, boot-stealing mud that this trail is famous for. A few
miles of that and we popped out on M-107 near the Lake of the Clouds overlook
to close the traverse.

## Logistics

Backcountry camping is $20/night per site (up to six people), reservable at
MiDNRReservations.com up to six months ahead, with permits picked up at the
visitor center. Most designated sites have bear poles — this is black bear
country, so hang everything scented. Dogs are welcome on a 6-foot leash. A
Michigan Recreation Passport (or nonresident equivalent) covers park entry,
and the point-to-point works best with two cars or a bike drop between the
Government Peak trailhead and the Lake of the Clouds overlook lot.
